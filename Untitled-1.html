The compacted bed of finely ground coffee creates hydraulic resistance. Even distribution and level tamping (approx. 15kg force) prevent water from finding paths of least resistance ("channeling").

Chapter 04 • Troubleshooting & Diagnostics

Interactive Shot Diagnostic Wizard

Select a symptom from your cup below. The e-book will diagnose the root cause and let you jump straight to the simulator with the corrected variables.

Symptom A

"My espresso tastes sharply sour and thin."

Diagnosis: Under-extraction. Water passed through the coffee puck too quickly (under 20 seconds), extracting acids before sugars had time to dissolve.

Symptom B

"It tastes hollow, dry, and ash-bitter."

Diagnosis: Over-extraction. The grind is too fine or water is too hot, choking the flow and pulling bitter tannins from the coffee fibers.

Symptom C

"The shot spurts sideways and tastes both sour & bitter."

Diagnosis: Channeling. Clumps in the coffee bed caused uneven pressure. Use a WDT distribution needle before tamping flat.
